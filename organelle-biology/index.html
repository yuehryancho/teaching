Organelle Biology

Bachelor Program of Biotechnology and Food and Nutrition, National Taiwan University · year 2

A eukaryotic cell is a city of compartments. This course takes each organelle in turn and asks four questions: how is it built, how do proteins and lipids get there, what does it do, and what happens when it fails?

We cover the nucleus, endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi apparatus, endosomes, lysosomes and vacuoles, mitochondria, chloroplasts and other plastids, peroxisomes, and lipid droplets, and finish with how organelles talk to each other and how biotechnology exploits them. Examples come from yeast, animal, and plant cells, with links to nutrition, disease, and food science throughout.

By the end of the course, you will be able to

  1. Describe the structure, composition, and main functions of each major organelle.
  2. Explain the logic of protein sorting: signal sequences, translocation machineries, and vesicle traffic.
  3. Compare the endosymbiotic organelles (mitochondria and plastids) with the endomembrane system in origin, genome, and import mechanism.
  4. Relate organelle quality control (ER stress, autophagy, mitophagy) to cell survival and disease.
  5. Interpret fluorescence and electron micrographs, fractionation data, and simple organelle proteomics.
  6. Give examples of organelle engineering in biotechnology and of organelle dysfunction in human nutrition and disease.
