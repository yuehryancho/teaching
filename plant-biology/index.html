Plant Biology

Bachelor Program of Biotechnology and Food and Nutrition, National Taiwan University · year 1

Plants feed us, clothe us, and run the planet's carbon cycle. This course gives first-year students a working map of how plants are built and how they work: from the plant cell and its unique organelles, through tissues and organs, to water and nutrient transport, photosynthesis, hormones, environmental sensing, reproduction, and evolutionary diversity.

Every week links a core concept to a plant on your plate or a tool in the biotechnology industry, and combines two lecture periods with one studio period for hands-on observation, problem solving, and short quizzes. The course prepares you for Organelle Biology (year 2) and Plant Lipids (year 3).

By the end of the course, you will be able to

  1. Describe the structure of the plant cell, the major tissue types, and the anatomy of roots, stems, leaves, flowers, seeds, and fruits.
  2. Explain how plants move water, minerals, and sugars, and how they fix carbon in C3, C4, and CAM photosynthesis.
  3. Outline how hormones and environmental signals (light, gravity, day length) control plant growth and development.
  4. Trace the life cycle of a flowering plant from pollination to germination.
  5. Place the major plant lineages on a phylogenetic tree and relate their features to life on land.
  6. Connect plant biology to crop improvement, plant-based foods, and biotechnology, and read a simple plant science figure critically.
