Introduction of Protein Homeostasis

Bachelor Program of Biotechnology and Food and Nutrition, National Taiwan University · years 3–4

Protein homeostasis (proteostasis) is the dynamic balance among protein synthesis, folding, quality control and degradation that sustains growth, development and stress survival. Its failure underlies crop yield loss, developmental disorders and human degenerative diseases.

This course introduces the nodes of the proteostasis network, from ribosomal translation, molecular chaperones and the ER and secretory pathway to ribosome- and protein-level quality control and degradation, and links them to biomedical and biotechnological applications, balancing fundamental mechanisms with real examples.

By the end of the course, you will be able to

  1. Describe the components and node functions of the proteostasis network, and compare its conservation and divergence across microbes, plants and humans.
  2. Explain the molecular basis of protein folding, misfolding and aggregation, and how molecular chaperones assist folding.
  3. Compare the major regulatory and quality-control pathways, including translational control, ribosome- and protein-level quality control, the unfolded protein response, and protein degradation.
  4. Connect proteostasis failure to crop yield loss and human disease, and recognize its biotechnological applications.
