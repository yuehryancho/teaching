Plant Lipids

Bachelor Program of Biotechnology and Food and Nutrition, National Taiwan University · year 3

Lipids make up every membrane in a plant, store the energy in every seed, waterproof every leaf, and carry signals that decide whether a plant grows, defends itself, or dies. This course follows plant lipids from fatty acid synthesis in the plastid to the oils in your kitchen: membrane glycerolipids, sphingolipids and sterols, storage and surface lipids, lipid signaling in stress and immunity, modern lipidomics, and the engineering of plant oils for food, nutrition, and industry.

The primary literature follows one research program: two decades of work from Yuki Nakamura's lab on how Arabidopsis makes, remodels, and uses its glycerolipids. Reading one lab's papers lets you watch a research question evolve, from a phosphate-starvation phospholipase to lipid-regulated flowering and the ER stress response. You read primary papers each week and present one in the final weeks.

By the end of the course, you will be able to

  1. Draw the major plant lipid classes and explain where in the cell each is made.
  2. Trace fatty acid synthesis, desaturation, and glycerolipid assembly through the prokaryotic and eukaryotic pathways, and explain how lipids move between the plastid and the ER.
  3. Explain how membrane lipid composition changes with temperature, phosphate supply, and stress, and why.
  4. Describe triacylglycerol synthesis, oil body formation, and lipid mobilization in seeds.
  5. Outline the main lipid signaling pathways (phosphatidic acid, phosphoinositides, oxylipins, sphingolipids) and their roles in stress and immunity.
  6. Read and critique a lipidomics dataset, and evaluate a plant oil engineering strategy for food or industrial use.
